## When You Need PDF to Excel

PDFs are great for sharing. They're terrible for editing.

Common scenarios where PDF to Excel is necessary:
- **Financial reports** — you need to run calculations
- **Invoices** — you need to sort and filter line items
- **Research data** — you need to analyze numbers
- **Bank statements** — you need to categorize transactions

Copying from PDF and pasting into Excel usually results in a mess. Everything ends up in one column.

## Why Manual Copy-Paste Fails

When you copy from a PDF:
- Tables lose their structure
- Numbers become text
- Formatting disappears
- Multi-page tables break

You spend more time fixing than working.

## How PDF to Excel Conversion Works

A PDF-to-Excel converter:
1. Reads the text in your PDF
2. Detects column and row positions
3. Rebuilds the table structure
4. Exports everything to `.xlsx`

The best converters keep:
- Column alignment
- Row heights
- Cell values (as numbers, not text)
- Multiple pages in one sheet

## What Works Well

PDF-to-Excel conversion works best with:
- **Text-based PDFs** (created from Word, Excel, or digital tools)
- **Clear tables** with visible borders
- **Simple layouts** without merged cells
- **Standard fonts** (Arial, Times, Calibri)

## What Doesn't Work Well

Conversion struggles with:
- **Scanned PDFs** (images of text) — needs OCR
- **Handwritten content**
- **Complex multi-column layouts** (newspapers)
- **Tables without borders**

## Tips for Best Results

1. **Use text-based PDFs** — not scans
2. **Check your PDF first** — is the text selectable? If yes, conversion will work
3. **Clean up after** — verify numbers are actually numbers
4. **Split large PDFs** — convert in chunks for accuracy

## Conclusion

PDF to Excel conversion saves hours of manual work. For clean, text-based PDFs, the results are excellent. For scanned documents, you'll need OCR first.
